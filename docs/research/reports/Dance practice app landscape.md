# Others built every piece, nobody built the whole

Every single feature of the user's app has a cousin somewhere, but no product, commercial or hobby, combines them, and three parts appear to have no equivalent at all. The first is a player that counts 1–8 in the user's own recorded voice over a real, beat-analysed song and calls figure names on the beat. The second is routines checked for timing breaks by start count and length. The third is lesson-video sections linked to specific figure variations. The idea of a *personal salsa/bachata move library* is not new, and it is getting crowded fast. A wave of solo-developer iOS apps launched in 2025–2026. The closest of them, **Flow State: Dance Library** (October 2026), already has separate salsa and bachata libraries and a "Move Map" of positions and transitions that shows "where gaps remain" ([feelflowstate.com](https://feelflowstate.com/)). Counting over real music is also a live business: **Dancetime** recognises songs and counts them in real time ([dancetimeapp.com](https://dancetimeapp.com/)). Outside dance the pattern repeats. BJJ has many position-graph apps that never drew an audience, and boxing has popular combo-callers that call on a timer, never on a beat. Chess (Chessable) shows that "a graph of moves you own plus spaced repetition" can be worth real money, but on the back of a content marketplace. The answer, then: the category has been done, the integration has not, and at least four people have independently started building parts of the same design. That tells you the itch is real. It also tells you that, so far, the itch has stayed personal rather than commercial.

## Salsa and bachata tools split into counters, courses and a new wave of move journals

The Latin-dance app market falls into three groups. The oldest and most popular are **rhythm and count trainers that generate their own music**. Salsa Rhythm builds a salsa band instrument by instrument, with an instructor voice counting On1 or On2 and 2/3 or 3/2 clave. It has **4.7★ from about 3.4K ratings**, the largest audience found in the niche ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/salsa-rhythm/id379868541)). BeatLab sells separate Salsa and Bachata Rhythms apps with recorded instruments you can mute and a voice to "find the 1" ([beatlab.app](https://beatlab.app/en/)). Neither touches the user's own songs.

The second group **counts over real music**, and this is where the user's song library and player have their nearest rivals. **Dancetime – Salsa Beat Finder** listens to music from any source and identifies the song by audio fingerprint. It then counts 1–8 with a voice and On1/On2 step animations, can mute chosen beats so you keep the count yourself, and has a tap-the-1 game. It shows **4.7★ from 102 iOS ratings**, ships updates regularly (v5.6 in October 2026), has bachata in beta, and depends on a catalogue of roughly 15–20K songs rather than analysing any file you give it ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dancetime-salsa-beat-finder/id6476049765)). **Cadencia** is the closest match to the user's tapped 1. You import your own tracks, "set the tempo and beat 1 yourself", mark sections, and a live count follows. It does no detection, and it lists "move vocabulary… choreography builder" as coming later ([cadencia.dance](https://cadencia.dance/)). **Dentro** maps the 8-count on bachata tracks and splits stems, at $8/month for 15 songs ([getdentro.com](https://getdentro.com/)). **Show Me the Counts** loops named song sections at 50–150% speed with AI stems, but counts nothing automatically ([showmethecounts.com](https://showmethecounts.com/)). An open-source Python counter even has a "This beat is the 1" button ([GitHub](https://github.com/selvadurai/salsaCountingProgramPython)). The academic SalsaAsst system found that general-purpose beat trackers struggle on salsa ([Stevens](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/salsaasst-beat-counting-system-empowered-by-mobile-devices-to-ass/)). That supports the user's design rule that ML downbeats are only a suggestion and the 1 belongs to the user.

**Move calling exists only over a metronome.** Ritmo: Dance Practice (iOS, June 2026, zero ratings) "calls out each move in a sequence" from a built-in salsa and bachata library at a tempo you set. Its listing mentions no real song ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/ritmo-dance-practice/6778009666)). The Rueda de Casino Call Simulator queues calls so they "wait for their place in the measure" at a set BPM, with no voice and no song ([ruedasimulator.com](https://ruedasimulator.com/)). Nobody found places calls on an analysed song's beat grid, and nobody counts in the dancer's own recorded voice.

The third group, and the one that matters most here, is **personal move journals, nearly all iOS-only and nearly all launched in 2025–2026**:

- **Flow State** (iPhone) is the nearest competitor. It has separate salsa and bachata libraries and combos cut from your own clips frame by frame. Its Move Map draws moves and positions, what leads into what, and "where gaps remain". It also has notes pinned to video timestamps, a social Discover feed, and Pro at $69.99/year ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/flow-state-dance-library/id6760927859)).
- **Salsa & Bachata: DanceLog** is a class journal with mastery buckets and weekly-minute goals ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/salsa-bachata-dancelog/id6801615017)).
- **Sweet Dance Moves** saves YouTube, Instagram and TikTok links as moves, keeping them as links rather than downloads ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sweet-dance-moves-library/id6749212354)).
- **Repertoire** claims spaced repetition that keeps moves "in rotation until mastered" and flags rusty ones ([myrepertoire.io](https://myrepertoire.io/)).

Almost all of these show "not enough ratings", so the niche is young and its traction unproven.

## The feature-by-feature picture: six parts taken, three still open

Set side by side, the user's nine features fall into two groups: those with a real competitor and those with none. The table gives the closest match found for each, across all the dance and non-dance research.

| User's feature | Closest existing match | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Today: drills scheduled by urgency from logged sets | Repertoire (spaced repetition, rusty moves); rhythm-review and stas-sl/salsa-moves (hobby, SM-2/Anki-like); West Coach Swing (level-matched WCS drills, streaks) | Concept exists; frequency-as-priority over typed drills is a variant, not a first |
| Figure library, variations, own videos, start count + length | Flow State, Dance Journal, DanceLib; only rhythm-review stores count durations | Library is common; **counts on figures are near-unique** |
| Position graph + gap report | Flow State Move Map; ballroom precedes/follows tables; BJJ graphs (GrappleMap, Matnet) | **Taken as a concept** by Flow State; a computed personal gap report is less clear |
| Routines checked for timing and connection | Ballroom Quest `check_routine` (hobby, fixed syllabus) | **No shipped equivalent** |
| Song library, ML beats, tapped 1 | Dancetime (catalogue), Cadencia (manual 1), Dentro (paid mapping) | Pieces exist separately |
| Player: own-voice count + clave + figure calls on a real song | Dancetime (count over songs), Salsa Rhythm (clave), Ritmo/Rueda Simulator (calls at a BPM) | **No equivalent found** |
| Lesson sections looped/mirrored/slowed and linked to figures | Loop/mirror/slow is everywhere (Dance.io, LeapNow, Steezy); Sentu turns phone videos into labelled loops | Controls are commodity; **section-to-variation links not found** |
| Saved YouTube video downloaded and kept | Sweet Dance Moves and Dance Journal store links or imports | **Server-side download not found** |
| Multi-user, browser + Android | Dance Journal, DanceLib on Android; most rivals iOS-only | Unusual in the niche, not unique |

Sources for the table: [Repertoire](https://myrepertoire.io/), [rhythm-review](https://github.com/Waidhoferj/rhythm-review), [salsa-moves](https://github.com/stas-sl/salsa-moves), [West Coach Swing](https://westcoachswing.com/), [Flow State](https://feelflowstate.com/), [Dance Journal](https://dancejournal.app/), [Ballroom Genie](https://www.ballroomgenie.com/), [GrappleMap](https://github.com/Eelis/GrappleMap), [Ballroom Quest](https://github.com/20Sam/ballroom-quest), [Dancetime](https://dancetimeapp.com/), [Cadencia](https://cadencia.dance/), [Dentro](https://getdentro.com/), [Ritmo](https://mwm.ai/apps/ritmo-dance-practice/6778009666), [Dance.io](https://apps.apple.com/at/app/dance-io-mirror-loop-slow/id6748628387?l=en-GB), [LeapNow](https://apps.apple.com/cd/app/id6771540435), [Sentu](https://sentu.app/), [Sweet Dance Moves](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sweet-dance-moves-library/id6749212354).

The gap report needs a careful note. Flow State's marketing says "see which transitions you have and where gaps remain", but its map is built from combos you have labelled in your own videos ([feelflowstate.com](https://feelflowstate.com/)). From marketing copy alone it is unclear whether it computes *missing* transitions from position compatibility, as the user's app does, or only shows sparse areas. The researchers who looked at it disagreed on this. Either way, the user can no longer claim the gap report as a new concept, only as a more formal version of one. Flow State also has no counts, no music, no drill scheduling, no lessons as objects, and no Android. Two technical differentiators stand out. One is timing-aware routines, where start count, length, timing breaks and whether a routine loops are checked against a personal graph. The other is saving a YouTube video so it becomes a real recording or lesson video with sections. Commercial apps likely avoid server-side YouTube downloading for licensing reasons. That is an inference, but it would explain why only a personal tool does it.

## Beyond Latin dance, ballroom formalised the graph and AI scoring took the money

In dance generally, the large platforms sell content, not organisation. STEEZY has 1M+ Android installs ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/dev/STEEZY/)) and charges $19.99/month ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/steezy/)). It offers speed, loop, mirror and multi-angle views, but only on its own classes ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/steezy-learn-dance-faster/id1296001664?uo=2)). Most of what was built in 2025–2026 is **AI pose scoring for K-pop and TikTok covers**: FYP Dance, LeapNow, STEPIN and Audition ME compare your body to the teacher's ([FYP Dance](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6759196501), [LeapNow](https://apps.apple.com/cd/app/id6771540435)). That is a different direction from the user's app, which has no pose analysis. The beat-aware video tools get closest to the user's beat grid. DanceBeat detects beats in a choreography video, splits it into measures and lets you navigate by 8-counts ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/co/app/dancebeat/id6757979636)). It does this to learn a fixed choreography, though, not to rehearse a partner routine over any song.

**Ballroom is the one partner-dance world where "what can follow what" is already formalised and sold.** Technique books list every figure's precedes and follows. Dance Central sells Waltz and Foxtrot routine builders limited to legal follow-ons ([dance-forums](https://www.dance-forums.com/threads/resources-on-designing-ballroom-choreography.53542/)). Ballroom Genie offers suggested precedes and follows across 250+ steps ([ballroomgenie.com](https://www.ballroomgenie.com/)). These are fixed syllabus graphs, not personal ones. The single closest whole-system match found anywhere is a hobby repo. **Ballroom Quest** stores 151 figures with beats and counts and `only_after`/`only_before` rules, validates routines (`check_routine`), suggests bridge figures, plans weekly practice from history, and analyses beats and bars in your own song in the browser ([GitHub](https://github.com/20Sam/ballroom-quest)). It has 0 stars. For West Coast Swing, Lindy hop and tango, nothing beyond course libraries, metronomes and printable pattern charts turned up ([WCS Pattern Progress Chart](https://www.westcoastswingonline.com/wp-content/uploads/Pattern-Progress-Chart-WCS.pdf)). The same goes for a personal, user-defined transition graph.

## Analogous fields show the graph never sells alone, but the daily hook does

The analogies outside dance tell the user more about *what makes such a tool used* than about novelty. **Brazilian jiu-jitsu has built the position graph again and again, and none of those apps found an audience.** GrappleMap models positions as nodes and transitions as edges and has 214 GitHub stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/Eelis/GrappleMap)). Matnet's interactive node map and Match Planner have **5 ratings after nine years** ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/matnet-jiu-jitsu-database/1175062894)). Grapla's "knowledge graph of… thousands of validated transitions" had 3 followers on Product Hunt ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/grapla)). Coaches still endorse flowcharting your game ([TNBJJ](https://tnbjj.com/linkedpages/gameplan.html)), so the graph has value as a way to think, but not as the reason people open an app. None of these reports gaps in *your own* repertoire.

**Combo callers are the commercial opposite.** Heavy Bag Pro calls boxing combinations during bag work and reports **1M+ Google Play installs**. Shadow Boxing Workout has 2,958 US ratings at $79.99/year ([aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=18596)). Calls during practice are clearly wanted and paid for. But every caller fires on a timer, because boxing has no musical count. Calling the next figure on the right count of a real song therefore has no precedent in combat sports either.

Music shows where the parts come from. Moises made beat and chord detection mainstream, claiming **30M to 75M users** depending on date and source ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/technology/ai-music-app-moises-tops-30-million-registered-users), [Apple](https://developer-mdn.apple.com/articles/moises/)). Its count-in is a click *before* the song, not a count through it. Loop-and-slow trainers are a mature category; Amazing Slow Downer even names "dancer" in its listing ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id910469258)). Practice journals like Andante cap out around 1.3K ratings and are lightly maintained ([SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/apps/andante-practice-journal/id1530262372)). Chess gives the strongest evidence that the user's core model, a personal tree of moves drilled on a schedule, can be valuable. Chessable's MoveTrainer was called one of the two most valuable assets in Chess.com's **~$82M purchase of Play Magnus Group** ([SportBusiness](https://www.sportbusiness.com/news/chess-com-to-acquire-play-magnus-group-in-82m-deal/), [Perpetual Chess](https://benjohnson.substack.com/p/perpetual-chess-link-fest-2-august)). But Chessable sold *grandmaster-authored courses* on that engine, and chess moves can be graded right or wrong, which dance figures cannot.

The science behind the Today screen is solid, though older. A meta-analysis of 63 studies found spaced practice beat massed practice with an effect size of **0.46** ([Donovan & Radosevich 1999](https://www.gwern.net/docs/spaced-repetition/1999-donovan.pdf)). For motor skills, **over 80% of studies** showed a benefit from distributed practice ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaPashlerVulWixtedRohrer-PB-2006.pdf)). Purpose-built spaced repetition for physical skills is almost absent: MemoRep is the main example ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/memorep)). That absence is probably because grading a physical rep is awkward. The user's approach of frequency as priority, driven by logged sets rather than graded recall, avoids that problem, and no research found argues for any particular interval scheme over a user-set one. Figure skating's IJS program builders, such as Skate Plan, validate a routine against rules ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/de/apps/skate-plan/6759615058)). Those are scoring rules from a governing body. The user's checks are physical continuity, which is closer to GrappleMap's pose graph.

## Dancers feel the problem, but many solve it alone and won't pay

The need is documented. A dancer-teacher describes the post-class pattern: "You only remember a small amount and instead keep repeating the same things you already knew". He prescribes almost exactly the user's model: list what can follow each handhold or position, and drill with spaced repetition ([The Confident Lead](https://confidentlead.substack.com/p/how-to-remember-salsa-moves)). A six-page salsaforums thread on remembering moves turns up Google Drive move lists, notes written right after class and a "mobile database" ([salsaforums](https://www.salsaforums.com/threads/how-do-you-guys-remember-your-moves.24823/)). On dance-forums, one dancer runs **a self-built database on a Linux home server with a web front end** linking lessons, notes and videos. That is essentially the user's architecture. The same dancer doubts anyone would pay, since "good enough" tools already exist ([dance-forums](https://www.dance-forums.com/threads/how-do-you-organize-and-practice-dance-tutorial-videos.53139/)). The hobby record says the same thing:

- **stas-sl/salsa-moves** is an Anki-inspired salsa catalogue with A/B/C variations and Thompson-sampling practice. It has 2 stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/stas-sl/salsa-moves)).
- **rhythm-review** has moves with count durations, patterns and SM-2. It was built on Svelte and Drizzle, strikingly close to the user's stack, and has 0 stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/Waidhoferj/rhythm-review)).
- **Repaso** is a bachata class-video clipper, also with 0 stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/abhinav-kipper/dance-ios-app)).

Technical dancers keep building this for themselves, and almost none of it spreads.

The money picture is modest. The biggest online dance-learning companies are small. STEEZY raised about $3M in venture funding ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/steezy/financials)), and its revenue is estimated anywhere from $5M to $25M ([Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/steezyinc)). Market-size reports for "online dance training" vary roughly sixfold, from $450M ([Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/online-dance-training-market)) to $2.9B ([Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/online-dance-training-market/)), and none of them is credible. The salsa/bachata scene is committed and travels: one calendar lists **523 festivals in the next 12 months** ([SalsaVida](https://www.salsavida.com/guides/festivals/)). Learners already pay about €230/year for teacher-run course libraries ([VdanceClub](https://vdanceclub.com/bachata-with-vdanceclub/)). A practice companion would therefore be an indie-scale product inside a niche inside a niche, not a venture-scale one.

These findings have limits. Reddit could not be searched or fetched, so community demand (upvotes, feature requests) is unmeasured. Most comparisons rest on store listings and marketing pages, not hands-on use. App stores were searched only through web search, so small Android-only apps may be missing. Flow State and several rivals are weeks old, so their capabilities could change quickly.

## Conclusion

The user's app is not a new *kind* of product. It is the most complete version yet of something that dancers, Latin-dance indie developers and BJJ practitioners keep reinventing. The analogues add a lesson the dance market alone does not show. Graph and library tools stay tiny everywhere, while the things that win daily use are audio-guided sessions (boxing callers), one hard capability (Moises's beat detection) or content (Chessable's courses). By that measure, the user's real asset is the **player that walks a routine over a real song, counting in the user's own voice and calling figures on the beat**, backed by timing-checked routines and fed by the Today queue. It is the one part with no precedent in dance or anywhere else found, and it is exactly the kind of daily hook that makes the graph and library worth maintaining.

Each piece the user has built is getting closer to being copied. Flow State already has the graph, Cadencia plans a choreography builder, Dancetime owns counting over real songs, and Ritmo calls moves at a BPM. Whatever stays distinctive will come from how tightly the pieces connect, not from any one of them. If the app ever goes beyond personal and friends' use, the evidence points to two moves. One is to lead with the musical player and timing rather than the library. The other is a teacher channel, where a school hands students recap sections already linked to figures. Sentu and StepTap are testing that idea for video, but nobody is testing it for a practice schedule.
