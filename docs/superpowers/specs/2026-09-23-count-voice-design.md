# The count voice — patterns, and your own recorded voice

Extends [`2026-09-22-salsa-app-design.md`](2026-09-22-salsa-app-design.md),
"Songs & player (phase 2)". That spec stands except where this one says
otherwise; its **Voice** section is superseded by this document.

## The problem

Phase 2b ships one spoken count: 1-2-3, 5-6-7, in Piper's Spanish, one clip per
word, one word per beat. Two things are wrong with it.

**It cannot go fast.** Spanish runs about 7–8 syllables a second at full tilt,
so "cinco" needs roughly 0.30 s to be said at all. A beat is 60/BPM:

| BPM | beat | full count? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 125 | 0.48 s | comfortable |
| 160 | 0.375 s | fine |
| 180 | 0.33 s | tight but real |
| 200 | 0.30 s | at the limit |
| 250 | 0.24 s | impossible, for any voice |

Measured on the shipped clips: at 250 BPM "seis" begins halfway through
"cinco", and — although both play to completion, nothing truncates them — the
second word masks the first. You hear "cin". **There is a wall at roughly
190 BPM**, and no recording, no encoding and no scheduling trick moves it. Past
it the only lever is saying *fewer* words.

**The pause is hard-coded.** `COUNT_CLIP` stores `null` for 4 and 8, treating
the salsa pause as a fact about the music. It is a fact about the *dance*: son
rests on 1 and 5 instead, and sometimes hearing every count is what you want.

A third thing is not wrong but is a limit: assembled single words sound like a
list being read. Over a four-minute song that is tiring in a way a person
counting is not.

## Decisions

Taken during brainstorming on 2026-09-23; the reasoning is kept because each
one closes off an alternative that will otherwise look attractive later.

1. **Fast songs are answered by thinning the count, not by speeding it up.**
   The wall is physical.
2. **Recorded phrases are a HALF BAR**, not a whole bar and not a whole song
   loop. A half bar is the natural unit of the dance: phrase A is counts 1–3,
   phrase B is 5–7, and the silent 4 and 8 give each phrase's tail a whole beat
   to ring out. There is no seam to hide. It also keeps the figure-call duck
   working — the player drops phrase B and speaks the move name over 5-6-7,
   exactly as today.
3. **Nothing loops.** One source node is scheduled per phrase, anchored to the
   detected time of its own count 1 or count 5. A free-running loop must match
   the song's tempo forever and cannot: at 250 BPM a 0.1 % error swallows a
   whole beat in four minutes. Anchoring every phrase makes drift unable to
   accumulate.
4. **Recording happens in the app, captured through Web Audio, not
   `MediaRecorder`.** MediaRecorder gives Safari MP4 and Chrome WebM, and — the
   fatal part — no time origin, so the recording could not be aligned to the
   click except by guessing. An `AudioWorkletNode` fed by `getUserMedia` shares
   the `AudioContext` clock with the click, so every beat's sample index is
   known exactly.
5. **Recorded takes are an overlay, never a replacement.** Any pattern or tempo
   without a take falls back to the Piper clips, so the ladder can be filled a
   few tempos at a time and the player is never broken while it is partial.
6. **Sparse patterns use single words, not phrases.** At 1-3-5-7 the words are
   half a second apart; there is no flow between them to preserve, so a phrase
   recording would buy nothing and cost a take per tempo.

## Part 1 — count patterns

No recording, no schema change. This is what makes a fast song usable, and it
ships on its own.

### The patterns

`COUNT_PATTERNS` in `src/lib/labels.ts`, positions in
`src/lib/scheduler/scheduler.ts` beside `CLAVE_POSITIONS`:

| pattern | counts spoken | for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `salsa` | 1 2 3 · 5 6 7 | today's behaviour, the default |
| `son` | 2 3 4 · 6 7 8 | son / on-2, the pause on 1 and 5 |
| `all` | 1–8 | learning a figure's timing |
| `odd` | 1 3 5 7 | fast songs — 0.48 s a word at 250 BPM |
| `ones` | 1 · 5 | very fast, or when the count is just a landmark |
| `one` | 1 | the bar line only |
| `off` | — | folds the existing on/off toggle in |

`son` speaks 2-3-4 and 6-7-8: the salsa shape one beat later, resting on 1
and 5.

### What changes

- **`COUNT_CLIP` gains `4: 'cuatro'` and `8: 'ocho'`** and stops being nullable.
  Which counts are silent moves out of the clip table and into the pattern,
  where it is a choice rather than a property of the music.
- **Two new clips**, `cuatro` and `ocho`, generated by the existing
  `scripts/make-clips.sh` and added to `CLIPS` in `labels.ts` (which is what
  `loadClips` fetches). Same pipeline, same natural length, ~5 KB each.
- **`Toggles.count` becomes `CountPattern`** instead of `boolean`, and `cuesIn`
  tests membership of a `Set` built from `COUNT_POSITIONS[toggles.count]`
  instead of testing a boolean and a null clip.
- **`Setup.svelte`** replaces the "Voice count" checkbox with a chip row. The
  stored value **must** go through the existing `pick()` validator. An
  unrecognised pattern from a stale `localStorage` would reach
  `COUNT_POSITIONS[...]` as `undefined`, and `for…of undefined` throws inside
  the 25 ms tick — which never surfaces as an error, only as a player that
  silently plays nothing. That exact bug already shipped once, for `clave`.
- **`player_json`** records `count` as a string rather than a boolean. No
  migration: nothing queries inside `player_json`, and old sets keep their
  boolean untouched.

### Open question, not decided here

The figure call sounds on count 5 of the preceding bar so the move starts on
the next "1" (`CALL_POS = 5`). Son breaks on 2, so a son dancer arguably wants
the call one beat later, over 6-7-8, with the move starting on 2. Leaving
`CALL_POS` fixed is the safe default; changing it per pattern is a decision for
after the patterns have been danced to.

## Part 2 — your own voice

### What gets recorded

Per pattern, per tempo, two takes:

| | salsa | son | all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| phrase A | counts 1–3 | counts 2–4 | counts 1–4 |
| phrase B | counts 5–7 | counts 6–8 | counts 5–8 |

`all` is the one pattern whose phrases abut, with no silent beat between them.
Its tail simply overlaps the next phrase's head; every count is its own source
node, so they mix. Nothing special is needed, but the recording must extend
past the phrase to carry that tail.

`odd`, `ones` and `one` are not recorded as phrases. They use single words —
the user's, if word takes exist; Piper's otherwise.

### The tempo ladder

Roughly every 8 %: **120, 130, 140, 152, 164, 177, 191**, and stop. Past ~190
the words do not fit, so a take there would be unusable however it was made.
Extending *downward* matters more than it looks: practice speed multiplies, and
a 180 BPM song at 0.7× is an effective 126.

Nearest-tempo selection then leaves a rate correction under 4 %, well below half
a semitone — inaudible even though `playbackRate` shifts pitch.

### Capture

In the browser, inside a user gesture:

- `getUserMedia({ audio: { echoCancellation: false, noiseSuppression: false,
  autoGainControl: false } })`. The browser defaults pump the voice and, worse,
  echo cancellation hears the click and starts ducking the voice to kill it.
- `MediaStreamAudioSourceNode` → `AudioWorkletNode` collecting raw frames.
- The click is scheduled on the **same** `AudioContext`, so beat times and
  sample indices share one clock. This is the whole reason for not using
  `MediaRecorder`.
- **Headphones are required**, and the UI says so. Through a speaker the click
  bleeds into the mic and lands in the take.
- Output: 16-bit mono WAV at the context's own sample rate. No resampling, no
  transcoding, no ffmpeg on the server (which has none), and `decodeAudioData`
  reads WAV everywhere. ~150 KB a take; the full three-pattern ladder is ~6 MB.

Safari has supported `AudioWorklet` since 14.1, so a current iPhone is fine.

### Pre-roll

If the user counts on the click, the **vowel** of "cinco" lands on the beat and
its /s/ starts ~80 ms earlier. Trimming at the beat would cut the consonant off.

So a take stores a **pre-roll**: audio kept before the phrase's first beat, and
playback starts the buffer at `beatTime − preRoll`. The user's own timing is
preserved rather than corrected — we are only declining to throw away the front
of the word.

This applies to the Piper clips too, which are currently trimmed hard to sample
zero and so may sit perceptibly late. Worth fixing there in the same pass.

### Recording screen

A route of its own (`/voice`), reached from the player's setup screen.

- A grid of pattern × tempo showing which cells are filled.
- Picking a cell: the headphone warning, one bar of count-in, then the click
  runs and the user counts **four bars**.
- Those four bars are sliced into four candidate A takes and four candidate B
  takes. The user auditions them against the click and keeps one of each.
  Recording four bars and choosing beats re-recording until one is clean.

### Data

One new table, following the existing conventions — integer epoch ms, the file
on disk under `$DATA_DIR`, the row owning its name:

```
count_takes
  id            integer primary key
  pattern       text     one of COUNT_PATTERNS
  bpm           integer  the click tempo it was recorded at
  phrase        text     'a' | 'b'
  file          text     unique; uuid + .wav under $DATA_DIR/count/
  sample_rate   integer
  pre_roll_s    real     audio before the phrase's first beat
  length_s      real     the phrase itself, beat 1 to the end of its last beat
  duration_s    real     the whole file, including the tail
  size_bytes    integer
  created_at    integer
  unique (pattern, bpm, phrase)
```

- **Upload:** raw-body `POST /api/count-takes`, mirroring the figure-recording
  endpoint's shape but without Range or streaming — these are ~150 KB. A small,
  separate byte cap belongs in `src/lib/limits.ts`; `MAX_RECORDING_BYTES`
  (95 MiB) is for phone video and is the wrong ceiling here.
- **Serving:** `GET /count/[file]`, like `/audio/[file]`. Private, like
  everything else — `hooks.server.ts` denies by default.
- **Deleting a take** is a hard delete, like a recording. Re-recording replaces.

### Choosing a take, and playing it

At the start of a run, once per run:

1. `effectiveBpm = bpm × speed` (0.7× playback makes a 180 song behave as 126).
2. Find the take set for the active pattern whose `bpm` is nearest in log space.
3. If nothing is within ~12 %, use the per-word clips instead. Better a Piper
   count than the user's voice pitched a semitone and a half.

Then, per phrase:

```
start  = timeAt(eight, firstCount) − preRoll / rate
rate   = length_s / (actual phrase duration from the grid)
```

`rate` absorbs the song's drift bar by bar, and because every phrase is placed
absolutely there is nothing to resynchronise.

`cuesIn` stays pure and knows nothing about takes. Phrases come back in their
own array rather than as a variant of `Cue`, the way `calls` already does —
less churn, and `cues` keeps meaning "a clip at a time":

```ts
cuesIn(...): { cues: Cue[]; phrases: Phrase[]; calls: Call[] }
// Phrase = { at, endsAt, half: 'a' | 'b' }
```

`attach.ts` resolves a phrase to a buffer and a rate; `endsAt` is what lets it
compute that rate without knowing the tempo. Whether a run emits phrases is
decided once, at start, from `Toggles.phrases`.

Two details the arithmetic has to get right:

- The rate is against **wall-clock** time, not song time:
  `lengthS / ((endsAt − at) / playbackRate)`. At 0.7× the song's own timeline
  is unchanged but the ear hears the bar take longer.
- The pre-roll is in the take's timebase, so it occupies less context time the
  faster the take plays: the source starts at `ctxAt(at) − preRollS / rate`.

The duck also gets coarser and simpler. A call owns 5-6-7, which is the whole
of phrase B, so the phrase is dropped outright rather than individual counts
being suppressed inside it.

A bar cut short by a re-anchor has no complete phrase; `timeAt` already returns
`null` there and the phrase is skipped.

## Out of scope

**Changing the pattern mid-run is IN scope and built** — a chip row beside the
voice slider, wiring up the `setToggles` that had sat unused since phase 2b. A
song that turns out too fast to count every beat wants thinning then, and
stopping would discard the figures already planned. The logged set records the
pattern in force when the run ended.

- **Input latency is not compensated.** The click's output time is known
  exactly, but nothing reports how long the microphone path delays what it
  captures, so a take can hold the voice a few tens of milliseconds later than
  it was spoken — and playing it back on the beat would then sound late. The
  cure, if an ear says it is needed, is a per-take nudge in the recorder rather
  than anything clever; the pre-roll already provides the room for it.
- Recorded **figure names**. They stay on `speechSynthesis` — a name sounds
  across three beats, so its jitter does not matter.
- Pitch-preserving **time-stretch** (a WASM library). The ladder plus a ±12 %
  fallback makes it unnecessary.
- Choreographies (phase 3).

## Risks

- **The voice may not be better than Piper.** The fallback means finding out is
  cheap: record three tempos of `salsa` and listen. Nothing else depends on it.
- **`getUserMedia` in a standalone iOS PWA** has historically been the flakiest
  part of this stack. Test from the installed app, not just Safari, before
  building the whole grid.
- **The constraints may be ignored.** iOS honours `echoCancellation: false`
  inconsistently. Headphones make it moot; the UI should insist rather than
  suggest.
- **A half-filled ladder** means the source changes between songs as the tempo
  crosses a threshold. The ±12 % rule keeps that from sounding broken, but it
  will be noticeable, and the grid should make the gaps obvious.
- **Abandoned halfway** is the likeliest outcome of any "record 42 takes"
  plan. Hence the overlay design: three takes is a useful amount of work, and
  nothing is wasted if it stops there.
